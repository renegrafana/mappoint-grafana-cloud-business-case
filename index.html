The Problem

Open source isn't free — it's differently expensive

Running Grafana, Prometheus, Loki, and Tempo in-house requires your team to provision infrastructure, manage upgrades and security patches, handle scaling, maintain high availability, and troubleshoot the observability stack itself — before they can use it to troubleshoot anything else.

When you factor in infrastructure costs (compute, storage, networking), engineering costs (the 2.4 FTEs maintaining the stack), and opportunity costs (what those engineers would otherwise build), self-managed OSS costs roughly 2.1× more than a managed service. Most organisations significantly underestimate this because infrastructure costs are spread across cloud bills and engineering costs are absorbed into team budgets rather than tracked as an observability line item.

A hybrid model — running some components on-premise and some in the cloud — reduces but doesn't eliminate this burden. You still carry the operational complexity of managing the on-premise components, coordinating versions between environments, and maintaining two sets of access controls and compliance configurations.

Five hidden costs of self-managed observability

  • Infrastructure sprawl: Self-managed stacks are over-provisioned by ~40% on average because teams size for peak rather than actual usage, and lack the tooling to right-size automatically.
  • Engineering drain: Upgrades, security patches, scaling, and troubleshooting consume 2.4 FTEs on average — capacity that could be building customer-facing features.
  • Compliance burden: OSS Grafana has no native SSO, SAML, LDAP, or fine-grained RBAC. Meeting SOC 2, HIPAA, or regulatory requirements requires custom configuration work, recurring audit effort, and ongoing maintenance.
  • Downtime exposure: A self-managed monitoring stack has no SLA. The median revenue impact of unplanned downtime is $9,200 per minute (all-industry). When the observability stack itself fails during an incident, resolution takes an additional 47 minutes on average.
  • Feature ceiling: OSS does not include AI-powered investigation, SLO management, Adaptive Telemetry, incident response workflows, Knowledge Graph, or enterprise data source integrations. Each of these either requires a separate paid tool or goes unsolved.

Automation & AI

Capabilities that only exist in Grafana Cloud

These are not incremental improvements over OSS — they are entirely new capabilities with no self-managed equivalent. Each one automates work that currently requires senior engineering time or goes undone.

GA

Grafana Assistant

A conversational AI built into the Grafana interface. Engineers query any connected data source in natural language — no need to know PromQL, LogQL, or TraceQL syntax. It creates and modifies dashboards, explains system behaviour, answers how-to questions, and onboards new team members by walking them through the environment. Integrates with Slack, GitHub, Jira, Linear, and 50+ tools via MCP.

→ Faster onboarding. Self-service for non-expert users. Reduced reliance on senior engineers for routine queries.
GA

Assistant Investigations

When an incident occurs, Assistant Investigations runs parallel AI analysis across metrics, logs, traces, and profiles — correlating the alert against recent deployments and incident history to propose candidate root causes. In production testing, it reached root cause 3.5× faster than manual on-call investigation (8 minutes vs. 28 minutes). It also identifies unbounded queries, misconfigured services, and resource exhaustion automatically.

→ Engineers open an investigation holding a hypothesis, not a blank dashboard. Dramatically reduces mean time to resolution (MTTR).
GA

Adaptive Telemetry

Four components — Adaptive Metrics, Adaptive Logs, Adaptive Traces, and Adaptive Profiles — that continuously analyse your actual usage patterns and recommend telemetry to aggregate, drop, or sample. Keeps the signals you actually query and alert on. Trims the ones you don't. In practice: Uni Cards reduced metrics from 4 million to 700,000 series, eliminating ~$12,000/month in cost. Lovable cut metrics volume by 93%.

→ Control observability costs automatically without sacrificing the signals that matter during incidents. No manual cardinality cleanup.
GA

Knowledge Graph

Two components: Knowledge Graph Entities automatically maps relationships between services, dependencies, and infrastructure as your environment changes. Knowledge Graph Memories retains operational context from past incidents and decisions so investigations don't start from scratch. Together they provide the "connective tissue" between telemetry signals — not just what's happening, but where it originated, what changed, and what downstream systems are affected.

→ Every agent and engineer starts from a live map of your systems. No manual service catalog maintenance. Context persists across incidents.
GA

Alert Enrichment

Automatically adds context to a firing alert before anyone looks at it — service relationships from Knowledge Graph, recent deployment history, and correlated anomalies. This means both Assistant Investigations and your on-call engineer start from a brief rather than a raw signal.

→ The alert itself becomes actionable intelligence, not just a notification that something is wrong.
Public Preview

Assistant Watchers

User-configured, periodic assessments of operational concerns. Watchers detect, assess, and can escalate issues before they trigger an alert — moving detection from reactive to proactive without anyone watching a dashboard around the clock.

→ Shift the metric from MTTR (how fast you respond) to interception rate (how many problems you catch before a user notices).

Migration Path

What the migration actually involves

Migration to Grafana Cloud is a configuration change, not a rewrite. Your applications and instrumentation stay the same.

StepWhat Happens
1. Repoint collectorsSend Alloy, Prometheus remote-write, and OTLP traffic to Grafana Cloud endpoints. This is a collector configuration change over standard protocols — no application changes.
2. Connect private data sourcesPrivate sources connect through Private Data Source Connect (PDC) via SOCKS over SSH. No VPN redesign required.
3. Import dashboardsExport dashboard JSON from OSS and import into Grafana Cloud. Remap data source UIDs as part of the import.
4. Validate in parallelRun OSS and Cloud side by side during a 30-day proof-of-value. Confirm data parity. Experience the Cloud-only capabilities (integrations, AI, Knowledge Graph) before decommissioning anything.
5. Decommission & scaleShut down the self-managed infrastructure. Onboard new teams without adding operational overhead. Grafana Professional Services can run the migration end to end.
